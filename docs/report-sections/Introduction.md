# Introduction

## Problem Statement
Nowadays, many networks are subject to cyberattacks in which attackers gain access and interfere with the network maliciously. Network intrusion is a well-known cybersecurity threat, and with ongoing technological advances, many machine-learning-based models have been increasingly used and extensively trained to distinguish between normal and malicious traffic. However, the performance of such models depends heavily on the quality of the data they are trained on, making them vulnerable to data poisoning and cyberattacks. One such attack is label flipping, in which an attacker manipulates the training data by switching the labels of some samples, such as labeling attack traffic as benign or benign traffic as attack. This can cause the model to learn incorrect classifications and patterns, affecting its performance by reducing its ability to identify malicious traffic accurately.

## Motivation
These reduced detection capabilities can have serious consequences for individuals and organizations, as network intrusion attacks may go undetected. Through these label-flipping attacks, other cyberattacks can bypass the system allowing for gain of unauthorized access to the system, steal sensitive information, disrupt services, and cause data breaches. Because of this, ML-based Network Intrusion Detectors need to be accurate and reliable so that organizations and individuals can use them efficiently without letting malicious intrusion attempts bypass detection. Therefore, analyzing the impact of label-flipping attacks on ML-based NIDS is crucial for understanding how vulnerable they are to these threats and how their performance and capabilities can be affected. This can further help us identify ways to make them more resistant to such attacks, improving robustness and reliability.
